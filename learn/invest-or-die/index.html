Model notesInvestment examples use a 6% annual return. Lifetime income is modeled with age-based career growth and 2% annual inflation. Future returns, income, taxes, laws, and contribution limits will vary.

Employer match

Your employer puts money into your retirement account when you contribute. This example models an employer contribution equal to 4% of pay from age 25 through 65.

Roth IRA

A Roth IRA is a retirement account you fill with money you've already paid taxes on. The boring-but-important number: in 2026 you can put in up to $7,500 per year if you're under 50, or $8,600 per year at 50+. This model assumes you eventually get enough adult money to max it starting at 40, with future limits rising with inflation.

Social Security

Today's average retired worker receives about $2,075 per month. Social Security is not projected to disappear, but under the 2026 Trustees' current-law projection the retirement trust fund would exhaust its reserves in 2032. Ongoing revenue would cover about 78% of scheduled benefits then, declining to 62% by 2100. Congress can change the program before that happens, so your exact future benefit is unknown. The current projection still points to a smaller safety net for your generation than today's scheduled benefits.

Retirement budget

This page uses a retirement spending range equal to about 55% to 80% of modeled final working income. That works out to roughly $8.1k to $11.8k per month in future dollars. The portfolio does not get emptied at retirement: it remains invested while withdrawals fund living expenses. This example uses a 4% first-year annual withdrawal rate, or about $8.3k/month from $2.50M and $13.4k/month from $4.02M. A 4% rate is a planning guideline, not a guarantee that a portfolio can never run out.
